PilotPathway.ai — Private Pilot ground school · 54 draft cards · prepared 2026-09-22

What we are asking you to do

These are multiple-choice cards for an AI-assisted ground school for students aged roughly 16 to 26, most of them new to aviation. No student has seen any of them. Nothing reaches a student until a CFI approves it by name, and that is what this packet is for.

For each card, please judge three things:

  1. Is it accurate? If a card is wrong, or right but misleading, say so. "Cut this one" is a useful answer.
  2. Are the wrong answers realistic? Each wrong option is meant to be a mistake a real student pilot makes. An obviously silly option teaches nothing — the student picks the right answer by elimination and learns that they are good at tests.
  3. Does the card test the objective it sits under? The objective is printed above each group of cards.

Two things we need you to fill in, marked in the text:

  • value needed — a number that is aircraft-specific, or one we were not certain of. We left a gap rather than guess. Please fill it in, or tell us the card reads better with no number at all. 9 cards carry one.
  • Our own doubt about this card — a specific question we have written down for you. 12 cards carry one.

The options are shuffled every time a student sees a card. The A–D letters here exist only so we can talk about them; no student sees this order. Each option also has a permanent id (opt-1 to opt-4) that does not change when the order does, so please use the id in any note about a specific option.

Two consequences of the shuffling, worth knowing if you rewrite an option: an option must never refer to another by letter, and there is no "all of the above" or "none of the above" — they depend on there being an above.

Sources are named, never numbered: no section numbers, ACS task codes, or figure numbers anywhere. A student who repeats a stale code to a DPE pays for our mistake.

Card 1

What IMSAFE is checking

s1-imsafe-pave.imsafe-checklist.c1

IMSAFE is a checklist you run on what?

  1. A The airplane's required equipment and paperwork opt-1
  2. B Yourself — whether you are fit to fly today opt-2 correct answer
  3. C The weather, the route, and the destination opt-3
  4. D The maintenance items due before the next flight opt-4

Shown after answering: Everything else you check before a flight is about the airplane or the conditions. IMSAFE is the one that looks at the pilot: Illness, Medication, Stress, Alcohol, Fatigue, Emotion. You may hear an instructor give the E as Eating — that version comes from other aviation authorities and some training groups rather than the FAA, so it is worth knowing it exists, but Emotion is the one we teach. It is the first real pilot decision of the day, and the only one nobody else can make for you. Confirm in the Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge.

Objective s1-pattern.pattern-legs · 3 cards

Card 10

Naming where you are

s1-pattern.pattern-legs.c1

You have flown past the end of the runway, turned toward it, and are descending — but you are not lined up with it yet. Which leg are you on?

  1. A Final opt-4
  2. B Base opt-2 correct answer
  3. C Downwind opt-1
  4. D Crosswind opt-3

Shown after answering: Base is the leg between downwind and final, flown roughly at right angles to the runway, and it is where most of the descent and the last look for traffic happen. Final is the leg aligned with the runway. Getting the names right is not vocabulary for its own sake: on the radio, the leg you say is how everyone else builds a picture of where you are. Confirm in the Aeronautical Information Manual and the Airplane Flying Handbook.

Card 11

Which way the turns go

s1-pattern.pattern-legs.c2

At an airport with no published exceptions, which way do traffic pattern turns go?

  1. A Whichever direction the wind favours that day opt-1
  2. B Right, so the runway stays visible from the left seat opt-4
  3. C Either, as long as you announce which one you are flying opt-2

Shown after answering: Turns in the pattern are to the left unless right traffic is published for that runway — and where it is, it is shown in the Chart Supplement, on the sectional, and on the airport's segmented circle. Card 13

Two of you on final

s1-pattern.right-of-way.c1

Two aircraft are approaching the same runway to land. One is lower than the other. What do the rules say?

  1. A The faster aircraft has the right of way, to keep the flow moving opt-3

Card 14

Converging on the way in

s1-pattern.right-of-way.c2

You are converging with another airplane at about the same altitude, and it is off to your right. Neither of you is landing yet. Who gives way?

  1. A You do — when aircraft of the same category converge, the one on the right has the right of way opt-2 correct answer

Shown after answering: When aircraft of the same category are converging at about the same altitude, the aircraft to the other's right has the right of way, so the airplane on your right keeps its course and you give way. Card 17

Deciding early

s1-pattern.go-around.c2

You are high and fast on final and telling yourself you can still salvage it. Why does the Airplane Flying Handbook press you to decide early?

  1. A Because a late go-around has to be reported opt-4

Shown after answering: The go-around itself does not get harder — your margins do.
