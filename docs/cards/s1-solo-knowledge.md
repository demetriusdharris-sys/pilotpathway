2. **Are the wrong answers realistic?**
3. **What should be cut?**

Markers in the text: **`[CFI: confirm value]`** is a number we would not guess. **`FLAG FOR CFI:`** is a specific doubt of ours, written down.

## About the A–D letters

**The options are shuffled every time a card is shown to a student.** The letters here are display only. Each option carries a stable id, `opt-1` to `opt-4`, and **Correct answer** names that id rather than a letter. Never write an option that refers to another by letter, and there is no "all of the above" or "none of the above".

The ids do not run in alphabetical order down a card. An option keeps its id when the options are reordered.

There is a blank **CFI review** line at the end of every card.

## Objective 1 — `s1-solo-knowledge.solo-endorsements`

> Describe the knowledge and endorsements required before solo flight

### Card 1 — The test before the test

**Objective ID:** `s1-solo-knowledge.solo-endorsements`

**Question:** Before your first solo, your instructor gives you a written knowledge test of their own. What is it for?

**Options:**

- **A.** (`opt-4`) It is the official knowledge test for the private pilot certificate
- **B.** (`opt-1`) It is optional practice, with no bearing on solo
- **C.** (`opt-2`) It is the required pre-solo knowledge test, covering the regulations, the airplane, and the airport you will be flying at
- **D.** (`opt-3`) It replaces the practical test later on

**Correct answer:** `opt-2`

**Explanation:** Before solo, your instructor administers a written test covering the rules that apply to your flight, the airplane's own procedures and limitations, and the airport and airspace you will be operating in — and they review any wrong answers with you. It is separate from the knowledge test you sit for the certificate, and it is one of the things that has to happen before you fly alone. Confirm in 14 CFR and with your CFI.

### Card 2 — What the endorsement is tied to

**Objective ID:** `s1-solo-knowledge.solo-endorsements`

**Question:** Your instructor endorses you for solo flight. What is that endorsement tied to?

**Options:**

- **A.** (`opt-1`) Any single-engine airplane at your school
- **B.** (`opt-4`) Any airplane, once you hold the endorsement
- **C.** (`opt-2`) The calendar year in which it was signed
- **D.** (`opt-3`) The make and model of airplane you were trained and tested in

**Correct answer:** `opt-3`

**Explanation:** A solo endorsement covers the make and model you were trained in, and it has to be kept current — it is not a permanent status and it does not transfer to a different airplane on the ramp. Flying a different type solo needs your instructor to train and endorse you for that type too. How often it must be renewed is in the regulation: `[CFI: confirm value]`. Confirm in 14 CFR and with your CFI.

### Card 3 — Going somewhere else

**Objective ID:** `s1-solo-knowledge.solo-endorsements`

**Question:** You want to fly solo to another airport. What does that need beyond your solo endorsement?

**Options:**

- **A.** (`opt-4`) Additional training and endorsement for solo cross-country flight, including for the specific flight you are planning
- **B.** (`opt-1`) Nothing further — a solo endorsement covers any flight
- **C.** (`opt-2`) Only a phone call to your instructor on the day
- **D.** (`opt-3`) Permission from the destination airport

**Correct answer:** `opt-4`

**Explanation:** Solo cross-country is its own step: training in planning and navigating the flight, an endorsement for solo cross-country generally, and your instructor's review and endorsement of the particular flight you have planned. That last part is deliberate — someone who knows the route, the weather, and you signs off that this specific flight is within what you can handle. Confirm in 14 CFR and with your CFI.

## Objective 2 — `s1-solo-knowledge.solo-limitations`

> Explain the limitations that apply to a student pilot flying solo

### Card 4 — Inside the box you were given

**Objective ID:** `s1-solo-knowledge.solo-limitations`

**Question:** Your endorsement names conditions — the airport, the weather, the time of day. What is their status?

**Options:**

- **A.** (`opt-4`) They are guidance, and a pilot in command may set them aside
- **B.** (`opt-2`) They are limits on the flight, and flying outside them is flying without a valid endorsement
- **C.** (`opt-1`) They apply only to the first solo flight
- **D.** (`opt-3`) They can be extended by the tower on the day

**Correct answer:** `opt-2`

**Explanation:** The conditions written on an endorsement are the flight the instructor authorised. Step outside them — a different airport, weather worse than stated — and the endorsement no longer covers what you are doing. Being pilot in command means you can always decide *not* to fly; it does not let you widen the limits you were given. Confirm in 14 CFR and with your CFI.

**Visual:** A logbook endorsement with its conditions written out, and beside it a map with a shaded area matching those conditions. A route is drawn staying inside the shading.

### Card 5 — The weather turned

**Objective ID:** `s1-solo-knowledge.solo-limitations`

**Question:** You are solo in the pattern and the weather is closing in toward the limits on your endorsement. What is the expectation?

**Options:**

- **A.** (`opt-1`) Continue until the limits are actually reached
- **B.** (`opt-4`) Climb above the cloud and wait for it to clear
- **C.** (`opt-2`) Fly to another airport without telling anyone
- **D.** (`opt-3`) Land while conditions are still comfortably inside your limits

**Correct answer:** `opt-3`

**Explanation:** A limit is not a target to fly up to. Landing early is a completely ordinary outcome, and it is what your instructor is hoping you will do — a student who came back early has made exactly the decision the training is meant to produce. Nobody is disappointed by that; they are disappointed by finding out you pressed on. Confirm in 14 CFR and with your CFI.

**Visual:** A pattern with an airplane turning early onto base and landing, with the weather drawn as a band still some distance away rather than overhead.

### Card 6 — Something is wrong with the airplane

**Objective ID:** `s1-solo-knowledge.solo-limitations`

**Question:** On a solo flight you notice something about the airplane that does not seem right. There is nobody beside you. What do you do?

**Options:**

- **A.** (`opt-2`) Complete the planned lesson, then mention it afterwards
- **B.** (`opt-1`) Try to diagnose it in flight before deciding
- **C.** (`opt-4`) Land as soon as it is safe to do so, and tell your instructor and the maintenance staff
- **D.** (`opt-3`) Say nothing unless it happens again

**Correct answer:** `opt-4`

**Explanation:** You are pilot in command, which means the decision is yours and the airplane comes back. Ending a flight early over something that turns out to be nothing is a good outcome that costs almost nothing; the alternative is the one that occasionally costs everything. Say what you saw plainly afterwards, even if it is hard to describe. Confirm in 14 CFR and the Airplane Flying Handbook.

**Visual:** A single airplane in the pattern with a shortened flight path curving back to the runway, and on the ground a figure talking to an instructor and a mechanic. Nothing dramatic is drawn in the air.

## Objective 3 — `s1-solo-knowledge.who-can-endorse`

> State who is authorized to endorse you and who is not

### Card 7 — Who may sign

**Objective ID:** `s1-solo-knowledge.who-can-endorse`

**Question:** Who can give you the endorsements you need for solo flight?

**Options:**

- **A.** (`opt-3`) The flight school's chief administrator, on the instructor's behalf
- **B.** (`opt-1`) An authorized flight instructor who has trained you and judged you ready
- **C.** (`opt-4`) Any certificated pilot who has flown with you
- **D.** (`opt-2`) A designated examiner, in advance of your training

**Correct answer:** `opt-1`

**Explanation:** Endorsements come from an authorized flight instructor — a person holding an instructor certificate, who has given you the training and is willing to put their own certificate behind their judgment about you. Not the office, not a friend who happens to be a pilot, and not an examiner ahead of time. Confirm in 14 CFR.

**Visual:** A logbook page with a single endorsement block and a certificate number written into it, and beside it three figures drawn at the same size — an office administrator, a private pilot, an examiner — none of them holding the pen.

### Card 8 — What an AI cannot do

**Objective ID:** `s1-solo-knowledge.who-can-endorse`

**Question:** Captain Path says your knowledge of solo requirements is solid. What does that mean for your solo flight?

**Options:**

- **A.** (`opt-2`) That your ground knowledge is in good shape, and the decision about solo remains entirely your instructor's
- **B.** (`opt-4`) That you are ready to solo
- **C.** (`opt-1`) That your instructor is expected to endorse you
- **D.** (`opt-3`) That you may solo once you have also passed the knowledge test

**Correct answer:** `opt-2`

**Explanation:** This app can tell you whether you have the knowledge straight. It cannot see you fly, it does not know how you handled a crosswind last week, and it is not permitted to judge readiness to fly alone — that judgment belongs to a certificated instructor who has been in the airplane with you. Anything else would be this product trading your safety for a claim it cannot support. Confirm in 14 CFR and with your CFI.

### Card 9 — Asking when you are not sure

**Objective ID:** `s1-solo-knowledge.who-can-endorse`

**Question:** You are not certain whether a particular flight is covered by your endorsements. What is the right move?

**Options:**

- **A.** (`opt-1`) Fly it, and check afterwards
- **B.** (`opt-4`) Ask another student what they were allowed to do
- **C.** (`opt-2`) Assume it is covered, since you were endorsed for solo
- **D.** (`opt-3`) Ask your instructor before you fly

**Correct answer:** `opt-3`

**Explanation:** Endorsements are specific, and the person who wrote yours can answer in a sentence what you might otherwise guess at. Asking costs a phone call. Guessing wrong means flying without a valid endorsement, which is a problem for you and for the instructor who signed. Confirm in 14 CFR and with your CFI.
